Four things only you can give the site.

The site is live and fast. Every picture on it today is a licensed stock photo of somebody else's esrogim. These four items turn it into yours. Send everything to joseph@rankfriendly.com.

1

Ten photographs

Open this page on your phone and work down the list. Twenty minutes, one afternoon, one table.

Before you start

  • Daylight near a window. No flash, no overhead fluorescent if you can avoid it.
  • Plain background: a white tablecloth or a clean wood table.
  • Wipe the phone lens first. Do not zoom; step closer.
  • Take every shot both horizontal and vertical.
  • Send the originals: email, Google Drive, or WhatsApp as a Document (not as a photo, which shrinks it).
  • JPG, not HEIC. iPhone: Settings > Camera > Formats > Most Compatible.
  • No women in frame (house style). Boys holding the Chinuch set are welcome.
  1. 1

    The three sets side by side, boxed

    Mehudar A-A, Mehudar A and Chinuch in a row on a plain table, labels facing the camera. Horizontal.

    Where it goes: Becomes the home page hero. Today the hero is a market table in Yerushalayim, not your sets.

  2. 2

    One esrog in its open box

    Lid beside the box, pitom up, shot from slightly above at a three-quarter angle. Daylight from a window, no flash.

    Where it goes: The product page for each level opens on this frame.

  3. 3

    The esrog in a hand

    An adult hand holding the esrog with the pitom toward the camera. Only the hand, no face needed.

    Where it goes: Shows real size; every buyer asks how big it is.

  4. 4

    The sealed lulav in its sleeve

    Full length, laid on the diagonal so it fits the frame, seal visible. Vertical.

    Where it goes: "Sealed" is a promise on every page; this is the proof.

  5. 5

    The hadassim bag, sealed

    Close enough that the leaves in threes are visible through the bag, label readable.

    Where it goes: Goes on the four-minim strip and the Chinuch page.

  6. 6

    The aravos bag, sealed

    Same as the hadassim: close, label readable, daylight.

    Where it goes: Completes the four minim with your own packaging.

  7. 7

    A Rav checking an esrog

    From the side, with the loupe or against the light. The esrog is the subject; the face may be turned away if the Rav prefers.

    Where it goes: "How it works" step one. Nobody else in the space shows the inspection.

  8. 8

    The seal going on

    The moment the sticker or seal is pressed onto the box or sleeve. Hands and product only.

    Where it goes: The single most convincing frame for "kasher v'yashar, one hundred percent".

  9. 9

    Cartons ready to ship, or stacked at a Beis Medrash

    Horizontal, whole stack in frame, room lights fine.

    Where it goes: The community pages show pickup day; a real stack beats a stock photo.

  10. 10

    The complete set opened out

    Esrog box open, lulav, hadassim bag, aravos bag, laid out on a cloth. Shot from above.

    Where it goes: "What you get", one picture, used on the confirmation and share cards.

2

The names of the Morei Hora'ah

We studied about fifty sellers. Not one names the Rabbanim who check its sets. Your whole offer rests on "selected and inspected by Morei Hora'ah"; printing who they are is the strongest trust signal available, and the site already has the slot ready.

For each Rav, if he agrees to be named:

  • Name exactly as it should print in English (for example: Rav Y. Cohen)
  • Name in Hebrew, for the Hebrew page
  • One line: his role and city (for example: Moreh Hora'ah, Yerushalayim)

If a Rav prefers not to be named, say so and the band simply stays hidden. Nothing is published without your word.

3

A short clip of the inspection and sealing

  • 20 to 40 seconds, phone held horizontal, steady (lean on the table).
  • A Rav checking an esrog, then the seal going on the box or sleeve.
  • Quiet room is fine; no music, no narration needed.
  • Send the original file (Drive or WhatsApp as a Document).

It plays in "How it works" only when a visitor presses play. Never on a loop.

4

Your own Hebrew wording of the three standards

The English standard on the site is your text, word for word. The Hebrew page carries our faithful rendering of it. If you have the standard written in Hebrew the way you would say it to a customer in Yerushalayim, send it and it replaces ours, word for word, the same day.

Three short paragraphs: Mehudar A-A, Mehudar A, Chinuch. WhatsApp text is fine.
